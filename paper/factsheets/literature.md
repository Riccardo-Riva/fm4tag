## How the code connects to the literature (checked in the code)

- **Augmentations and losses:**
  - The fm4tag code cites SCARF (`src/fm4tag/augmentations/feature_dropout.py:12`), SAINT section 3.2 for CutMix (`augmentations/cutmix.py:16`) and SupCon (`losses/contrastive.py:29-30`, `MultiViewSupConLoss`).
  - The metrics in `metrics/uniformity.py` and `metrics/effective_rank.py` cite Wang & Isola (2020) and Roy & Vetterli (2007).
  - In salt, `salt/models/ssl.py` follows SAINT and CLIP (a symmetric, cross-view InfoNCE). It also offers the DCL option (Yeh et al., arXiv:2110.06848), the SupCon option, and cites CPC / InfoNCE (Oord). Its views are `Identity` and `SaintCorruption` (CutMix + Mixup) (`salt/configs/ssl/GN2.yaml`).
- **Optimiser and schedule:**
  - fm4tag on `main` uses AdamW with a linear warmup and a cosine decay to 0 (`modules/pretrain_module.py:375-389`, `modules/finetune_module.py:386-418`).
  - The SSL-campaign worktree `/storage3/DSIP/rriva/research/fm4tag-chunk` adds a WSD option: `finetune.schedule`, `decay_frac: 0.2` (`configs/default.yaml:272`). It was used in the 2026-11-16 W_measure_sweep runs (`slurm/ssl_campaign/W_measure_sweep_7126988/.../.hydra/config.yaml:259`, `schedule: wsd`).
  - salt GN2 uses AdamW with OneCycleLR (`salt/modelwrapper.py:929`; `lrs_config` initial 1e-7, max 5e-4, end 1e-5, pct_start 0.01 in `salt/configs/ssl/GN2.yaml`).
- **Attention and stability:**
  - QK-norm is on for every SSL-campaign arm (`fm4tag-chunk/src/fm4tag/configs/campaign_ssl.yaml:34`, `row_qk_norm: true`). The config comment says "without it contrastive pretraining grew the track row-attention logits to 1e5-1e7 (one-hot attention) and the 30-epoch runs blew up". This is the attention-logit-growth failure described by Dehghani et al. (2023) and Wortsman et al. (2024).
  - Gradient clipping is set to 1.0 in the pretrain arm (`configs/arm/pretrain.yaml:24`).
  - fm4tag sends attention through FlashAttention-2 (`models/attention.py:7-45`). salt GN2 uses `attn_type: flash-varlen`.
  - The ISAB / inducing-point row attention is in `models/attention.py:186` (`InducedRowAttention`).


## Review of the September plan (`/storage3/DSIP/rriva/research/fm4tag/paper/plan_v0.tex`) against the literature

The plan's Related Work list has six items: transformer taggers; jets as tabular data; tabular ML / foundation models; contrastive FMs; contrastive learning in jet physics; strong, vision-like augmentations. Its Background list has four: FMs, the contrastive loss, alignment/uniformity, effective rank. These are all supported by the literature below. Five bodies of work are missing for the two pillars the paper now has.

1. **Transfer learning: frozen vs fine-tuned vs from scratch.** This underpins the SC/FR/FT pillar. The plan's training-strategy list has these arms but cites nothing for them.
   - Yosinski2014Transferable: features become less transferable in higher layers, so frozen and fine-tuned layers behave differently.
   - Kumar2022FTDistorts: full fine-tuning can distort pretrained features; linear probing followed by fine-tuning (LP-FT) is better. This motivates FR and the staged unfreeze (`unfreeze_at_epoch: 3`).
   - He2019RethinkingPretraining: with enough labelled data and a long enough schedule, training from scratch matches pretrain+fine-tune. This is the closest published parallel to the finding "pretraining never beats SC; the gap shrinks with labels; gap = dataset size".
   - Newell2020SSLUseful: the benefit of SSL pretraining shrinks as the number of labels grows.
   - Rubachev2022TabularPretraining and Levin2023TabularTransfer: the same questions for tabular deep learning.
   - In HEP: golling2024masked (MPM, which gains most at small label fractions), vigl2024finetuning, Mikuni2025OmniLearn, Bhimji2026OmniLearned, Birk2024OmniJet, Harris2025Resim.
2. **Multi-task CE + SSL as an auxiliary loss.** This underpins the CE+JC pillar.
   - Zhai2019S4L: supervised loss plus a self-supervised loss, trained jointly.
   - Hendrycks2019SSLRobustness: an auxiliary SSL loss improves robustness and OOD detection, which links to the data-MC outlook.
   - Islam2021BroadStudy: a joint supervised + self-supervised contrastive objective transfers better than either alone.
   - Gunel2021SCLFinetune: CE + SupCon during fine-tuning.
   - Vigl2026Scaling (arXiv:2610.06784, on ATLAS JetSet2 with salt): auxiliary objectives lower the primary jet-classification loss at equal compute. This is the most directly comparable HEP result.
3. **View strength.** This is the theory behind the lambda scan.
   - tian2020makes (InfoMin): views should share only task-relevant information, and performance is reverse-U-shaped in view strength. It is already in diary.bib but not mentioned in the plan.
   - Chen2020SimCLR: augmentation strength and composition are decisive.
   - Chen2021FeatureSuppression and Robinson2021Shortcuts: easy shared features can dominate the contrastive solution. This is relevant whenever jet-level pT/eta are left unchanged between views. A later project's chat notes this risk for jet pT/eta (embedding2tagging transcript 99b51a9d).
4. **Representation-collapse metrics beyond uniformity and effective rank.** Garrido2023RankMe (effective rank predicts downstream performance), Jing2022DimCollapse (dimensional collapse) and Hua2021FeatureDecorrelation. Together they justify logging the effective rank of the aggregator embedding.
5. **Training-stability literature for intersample attention.** Henry2020QKNorm, Dehghani2023ViT22B, Wortsman2024SmallScaleProxies and Zhai2023EntropyCollapse. These are needed because QK-norm was switched on for every SSL-campaign arm after the logits blew up.

Items to update or tighten in the plan:

- **GN2 is now published.** It appeared in Nature Communications 17 (2026) 541 (DOI 10.1038/s41467-025-65059-6), so it should no longer be cited as arXiv-only.
- **GN3 has only public plots.** Its only public source is ATLAS-FTAG-2025-01 (public plots, 15 Aug 2025: tracks + neutral PFlow + lepton ID, six classes, Lion optimiser, geometric loss weighting). The plan's "addition of PFlow and electron features in GN3" should cite that page. A web-search summary of a CDS listing names the PUB note ATL-PHYS-PUB-2026-001 as restricted.
- **CMS reference.** CMS-DP-2025-081 is "Flavour tagging performance of the updated Unified Particle Transformer algorithm with the CMS experiment at sqrt(s)=13.6 TeV" (UParT v2, ParT-based; CDS 2948917). Pair it with Qu:2022 (ParT) and Sarkar2024CMSRun3 (arXiv proceedings).
- **"Physics-informed augmentations / IR safety"** (plan item 5) is exactly Dillon2022JetCLR ("Symmetries, safety, and self-supervision", which is the JetCLR paper itself, not a separate one). The data-MC part is Sheldon2025MACK, Harris2025Resim and Stein2022Adversarial.
- **"BDTs are the standard"** for tabular data: cite Grinsztajn2022TreesTabular, ShwartzZiv2022TabularDL and Chen2016XGBoost.
- **Tabular foundation models:** Hollmann2023TabPFN, Hollmann2025TabPFNNature, Qu2025TabICL and vanBreugel2024TabularFM, with the surveys Jiang2025TabularSurvey and Somvanshi2024TabularSurvey.

## Novelty statements and how far the searches support them

- **"No SSL / contrastive pretraining has been published for ATLAS/CMS b/c-jet flavour tagging."** I found none in two web searches and in arXiv abstract searches (`all:"jet tagging" AND contrastive`, `all:"b-tagging" AND "self-supervised"`).
  - The closest works are Sheldon2025MACK (contrastive learning for robustness to mismodelling, on boosted-jet tagging), Harris2025Resim (resimulation SSL, jet tagging), Chen2025RepLearningEval (contrastive vs MPM vs generative pretraining on a shared jet encoder; JetClass-type tasks) and Stamenkovic2026LHCbSSL (SSL for heavy-flavour decay maps at LHCb).
  - Wording: "to our knowledge", plus a final check before submission.
- **"Intersample (row) attention has not been used in HEP taggers."** An arXiv abstract search for "intersample attention" and "inter-sample attention" returned 0 HEP papers, but that search is narrow (abstracts only). Treat as likely, not certain.
- **"Per-feature tokenisation (FT-Transformer/SAINT) for track features."** I did not search this specifically. GN1/GN2/ParT embed the whole track vector into one token. The plan's footnote states this contrast, and it is supported by the GN2 and ParT papers.

## Inconsistencies and things a referee could attack

1. **JetSet size.** The poster says "~190M jets". The CERN Open Data record 93940, as summarised by the fetch tool, lists 48,698,675 events in three HDF5 files (100.4 GiB) of about 5.6M, 25.6M and 168M jets, which sums to about 199M. The total is not stated on the page. Recount from the files or state the number of jets actually used per split. The fm4tag-chunk config says pretrain 39.7M, train 18.6M, val/test 1.86M each, which is about 62M jets after preprocessing.
2. **GN2 citation.** The poster cites arXiv:2505.19689 only. The paper is now Nature Commun. 17 (2026) 541, doi:10.1038/s41467-025-65059-6 (arXiv journal_ref + CrossRef).
3. **JetCLR.** The computed task lists "JetCLR (Dillon et al. 2021)" and "Symmetries, safety, and self-supervision" as two references. They are the same paper: arXiv:2108.04253, SciPost Phys. 12, 188 (2022). Cite it once.
4. **Wrong salt arXiv ID.** The salt JOSS paper has no arXiv version that I could find. arXiv:2405.07580, which I first guessed, is an unrelated paper (DynLLM). Cite doi:10.21105/joss.07217 (JOSS 10(112) 7217, 2025; 16 authors, first author Jackson Barr per CrossRef). The JOSS landing page shows only three names in the search snippet; the full list is from CrossRef and the JOSS page fetch.
5. **Training details depend on the codebase and branch.**
   - QK-norm and WSD exist only in `fm4tag-chunk`. `row_qk_norm: true` is set for all SSL-campaign arms. WSD was seen in the 2026-11-16 W_measure_sweep runs; `schedule: cosine` is the default.
   - fm4tag `main` (the lambda scan) uses AdamW with warmup + cosine.
   - salt GN2 uses AdamW with OneCycleLR, and its config has no QK-norm. `do_qk_norm` defaults to False (`salt/models/attention.py:256`) and is switched on only by `norm_type: hybrid` (`salt/models/transformer.py:349-351`). `salt/configs/ssl/GN2.yaml` leaves the default `norm_type: pre`.
   - The paper must say which schedule and normalisation each experiment used. Otherwise a referee can attribute differences between the fm4tag and salt scans to the optimiser setup.
6. **Duplicate keys in the author's bibs.**
   - ParticleNet: Qu_2020 in diary.bib vs Qu:2020 in salt paper.bib.
   - Deep Sets: zaheer2018deepsets vs Zaheer:2017.
   - Transformer: vaswani2023attentionneed vs 2017arXiv170603762V. The diary entry also has year 2023, which is the arXiv revision date; refs.bib corrects it to 2017, NeurIPS 30.
   - GN2X: atlas2023transformer vs GN2X.
   - salt paper.bib's key `ATLAS:2019` is the 2022 arXiv / 2023 EPJC DL1r paper (EPJC 83, 681), so the key name is misleading. It is kept only for reuse.
7. **SAINT is arXiv-only.** It has no peer-reviewed main-track venue (I did not check its workshop version). Since it is the central architectural reference, cite it as arXiv:2106.01342. FT-Transformer (NeurIPS 2021) and NPT (NeurIPS 2021) are the peer-reviewed anchors for per-feature tokens and attention between datapoints.
8. **GN3 is public only as plots.** ATLAS-FTAG-2025-01 (15 Aug 2025) is public. The PUB note number ATL-PHYS-PUB-2026-001 comes from a web-search summary of a CDS listing marked restricted. Do not cite the note until it is public.
9. **CMS UParT v1 note.** I did not verify the first UParT note (CMS-DP-2024-066, seen only in conference slides), so it is not in refs.bib. Use CMS-DP-2025-081 plus arXiv:2412.05863.
10. **Semantic Scholar mismatches.** It mapped Le-Khac et al. to arXiv 2010.10144 and Banfi et al. to hep-ph/0605270. The arXiv API returns those titles under 2010.05113 and hep-ph/0601139, which is what refs.bib uses. Spot-check before submission.
11. **Novelty claims rest on limited searches.** "First SSL for ATLAS/CMS flavour tagging" and "first intersample attention in HEP" rest on a few web and arXiv-abstract searches. Phrase them as "to our knowledge".
12. **The poster's reference [1] wording.** It describes taggers that "map ... to a latent representation". Fine, but GN2's own paper does not present embedding-quality metrics, so the claim that embedding quality "is rarely monitored" is the author's and should be argued, not cited.
